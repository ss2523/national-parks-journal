Act I: Glacier National Park

August 21

The Unplanned Midnight Run & First Light

Some journeys don't begin with a quiet sunrise; they throw you straight into the deep end. Our flight into Bozeman was a forewarning of the wild ride ahead—the plane hit the tarmac so hard it bounced back into the air before settling. Shaken but eager, we made it to our motel around midnight, only to discover our reserved room had been given away. Stranded in the middle of the night after fruitless calls to packed hotels, we made a bold call: Let's drive straight to Glacier right now.

At 1:00 AM, we hit the pitch-black highway. Two hours in, a massive elk appeared out of the dark right in front of our car. A flash of dark eyes, a slam on the brakes, and an unavoidable impact. Driven by pure adrenaline, we kept pushing forward into the night. It wasn't until an hour later under rest-stop lights that we saw the full reality: our entire front bumper was completely crushed. Exhausted and shocked, we reclined the seats for a few hours until the deep indigo sky outlined the giant mountain silhouettes ahead.

We secured a vehicle swap at the Missoula airport just as the sun broke over the horizon. After grabbing breakfast and securing an early check-in around 11:00 AM, we washed off the exhaustion. By noon, refreshed and undefeated, we set out toward the gates of Glacier National Park.

🏔️
Refusal to give up — watching sunrise over Missoula after surviving our midnight drive.
August 22

Cedar Canopies, Alpine Waters, and Home-Cooked Comfort

Passing through the West Entrance, our first stop was the Apgar Visitor Center before heading toward the Trail of the Cedars. Stepping onto that raised wooden boardwalk felt like entering a green sanctuary—wrapped in ancient giant red cedars and hemlocks with the rush of Avalanche Creek echoing nearby.

Continuing along the Avalanche Lake Trail, the path wound upward along the turquoise gorge until opening up to pure magic: framed by steep mountain walls, distant waterfalls cascaded straight into crystal-clear alpine waters.

From there, we drove up toward Logan Pass. Finding a quiet spot amidst the crisp alpine air, we unboxed our packed lunch from home—homemade chicken fry and hot, tangy tomato rasam. Eating a comforting home-cooked meal high up on the Continental Divide made for an unforgettable memory. We finished our day resting at Lake McDonald, sipping local Going-to-the-Sun Golden Ale from the porch as night fell.

🌲
Enjoying tomato rasam and chicken fry with a 360° Continental Divide view.
August 23

The Road to Heaven, Mountain Goats, and Huckleberry Sweetness

We set out along the Going-to-the-Sun Road, passing McDonald Falls and driving through hand-carved stone tunnels into increasingly grand landscapes. Reaching Logan Pass, we hiked toward Hidden Lake Overlook—a wooden stairway straight into heaven surrounded by snow-dusted peaks.

Just as we took in the view, two fluffy mountain goats stepped onto the trail right ahead of us before taking off into a nimble run. As soon as the goats started running, everyone on the trail instinctively started running too! It was a hilariously chaotic, shared moment of pure joy high up on the mountain ridge.

After hiking out to St. Mary Falls, Virginia Falls, and Sun Point, we wrapped up our park loop at the visitor center and treated ourselves in West Glacier to our very first taste of huckleberry ice cream—sweet, tart, and extraordinarily yummy.

🐐
The exact moment the mountain goats took off running down the Heavenly Trail!
August 24

Emerald Lakes, Bear Country Claps, and the Long Road South

We drove to the historic Many Glacier Hotel on the edge of Swiftcurrent Lake and set off for the Grinnell Lake Hike. Along the way, we crossed a wooden suspension bridge swinging high above a rushing glacial stream, bouncing with every step!

Reaching Grinnell Lake was breathtaking—the water glowed in a striking opaque turquoise beneath massive mountain walls. Dipping our bare feet straight into the icy water was a shocking but invigorating thrill. On the narrow return loop through dense forest, we spotted fresh black bear scat directly on the single-file path.

We immediately sprang into action: clapping loudly, singing at the top of our lungs, and playing music from my phone until we safely broke out of the tree line back to the lodge! We celebrated with fresh pizza before embarking on the 7.5-hour drive south toward Victor, Idaho, pulling in late for a well-earned rest.

💧
Freezing glacial waters & 100% worth it — Grinnell Lake.

Act II: Grand Teton National Park

August 25

Panoramic Peaks, Clear Lakes, and a Mountain Feast

We began our exploration at Mormon Row, where rustic weathered barns stood dramatically framed by the massive Teton Range. We pulled over at Glacier View Turnout, Schwabacher Landing, and Snake River Overlook—standing right where Ansel Adams captured his legendary photographs.

After navigating up to Signal Mountain Summit for 360-degree views across the valley, we walked the shore of String Lake—a surprising highlight with glassy, shallow waters so clear you could see straight to the bottom. We wrapped up the day strolling through Jackson Village under the iconic elk antler arches before cooking a lavish feast of hot Indian vegetarian dishes.

🏔️
Classic Tetons reflection at Schwabacher Landing.
August 26

Sunrise Magic, Mountain Heights, and Fireside Evenings

Alarms went off well before dawn, and we arrived at Oxbow Bend just in time. As the first sunlight hit Grand Teton, the peak glowed in soft gold, casting a flawless mirror reflection across the Snake River.

After breakfast at Dornan’s, we took the shuttle boat across Jenny Lake and hiked up to Hidden Falls and Inspiration Point (7,200 ft elevation), taking in sweeping views across the entire valley. By late afternoon, we settled into our resort near West Yellowstone, grilling chicken over open flames and sipping cold Jenny Lake beers under the open evening sky.

🌅
7,200 feet up overlooking Jenny Lake.

Act III: Yellowstone National Park

August 27

Steaming Geysers, Turquoise Pools, and Fairy Falls

On our first morning in Yellowstone, wild elk grazed along the Madison Riverbank in the morning mist. We hiked the Fairy Falls Trail to the Grand Prismatic Overlook, taking in the hot spring glowing in vivid rings of fire-orange, gold, and deep indigo. Right in the parking lot, we devoured a delicious packed lunch of homemade Bagara rice and chicken curry.

We visited Old Faithful to watch its massive eruption, explored Black Sand Basin and West Thumb Geyser Basin, and returned home to grill corn and chicken while enjoying local Old Faithful beers.

🌋
Bagara rice & chicken curry right next to Grand Prismatic Spring!
August 28

Cozy Rain, Endless Bison, and the Best Biryani

A soft, steady rain fell throughout our long drive out to Lamar Valley. Endless herds of bison roamed across rolling green hills and crossed the roads right in front of us throughout the entire day.

Returning home by evening, we cooked Chicken Dum Biryani from scratch. Eating what turned out to be the absolute best Biryani of the trip, we talked about how fast time had flown, full and content before a sound sleep.

🦬
Bison traffic jams in cozy, rainy Lamar Valley.
August 29–30

The Grand Finale, Roaring Falls, and Sunset Farewell

Our final day began at the Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone. Standing at Artist Point, the view was breathtaking. We walked down to the Brink of the Upper Falls, standing right beside the roaring, thunderous water while enjoying our leftover Biryani for lunch!

We continued through Hayden Valley, Mud Volcano, and Mammoth Hot Springs before reaching the historic Roosevelt Arch. Stopping at a local shop, we treated ourselves to a huckleberry croissant jam—the single best sweet treat of the trip.

Exiting through the North Entrance around sunset, we took in a winding creek route under golden light, enjoyed Thai fried rice in Livingston, and packed up for our flight home—completely refreshed after an unforgettable journey immersed in nature.

🏛️
Eating Biryani at the Brink of the Upper Falls — untamed memories forever.
