From there, we drove up toward Logan Pass, where the world opened up into endless, sweeping mountain panoramas[cite: 3, 4]. As late afternoon shifted into evening, we made our way to the shores of Lake McDonald[cite: 3, 4]. Sitting by the edge of the water as the day gently quieted down was effortlessly pleasant and calm[cite: 3, 4]. The sky softened into shades of pastel, casting a warm glow across the mountains[cite: 3, 4]. The Road to Heaven, Mountain Goats, and Huckleberry Sweetness

We set out along the Going-to-the-Sun Road, passing the roaring cascades of McDonald Falls[cite: 3, 4]. Driving through the hand-carved stone rock tunnels felt like passing through portals into increasingly grand landscapes[cite: 3, 4]. We paused to marvel at the cool mist of the Weeping Wall straight onto the road and caught sight of the elegant cascade of Bird Woman Falls plunging down a distant granite face[cite: 3, 4].

Reaching Logan Pass, we set off on the trail toward Hidden Lake Overlook[cite: 3, 4]. The hike felt like walking a wooden stairway straight to heaven—surrounded by vast alpine meadows and towering, snow-dusted peaks[cite: 3, 4]. Just as we were taking in the majestic sight of Hidden Lake glowing from high above, two fluffy, magnificent mountain goats stepped onto the trail right ahead of us[cite: 3, 4]. They stopped for a quiet moment, observing the crowd before breaking into a nimble run down the path[cite: 3, 4]. As soon as the goats started running, everyone on the trail instinctively started running too![cite: 3, 4] It was a hilariously chaotic, shared moment of joy high up on the mountain ridge[cite: 3, 4].

Continuing past the pass, we hiked out to the roaring waters of St. Mary Falls and Virginia Falls, followed by the Sun Point Nature Trail overlooking St. Mary Lake[cite: 3, 4]. On our way back along the trail, a couple of playful hikers shouted that there was a bear ahead[cite: 3, 4]. Panic instantly surged—we turned on our heels and sprinted all the way back toward the parking area![cite: 3, 4] Once we realized it was just a lighthearted prank, we laughed off the adrenaline rush, turned back around, and claimed our spot on the sunlit rock edge to soak in the views[cite: 3, 4].

We wrapped up our park loop at the St. Mary Visitor Center watching an educational film about the history of Glacier, and stopped in West Glacier for our very first taste of huckleberry ice cream—sweet, tart, and extraordinarily yummy[cite: 3, 4]. After picking up ingredients at Smith's grocery, we returned home, grilled for a bit, cooked egg curry, and relaxed in front of the TV before bed[cite: 3, 4].

The Emerald Lakes, Bear Country Claps, and the Long Road South

Check-out morning marked our final hours in Glacier National Park[cite: 3, 4]. We drove across the park to the eastern side, arriving at the historic, Swiss-style Many Glacier Hotel on the edge of Swiftcurrent Lake[cite: 3, 4]. Setting off on foot for the Grinnell Lake Hike, the trail wound past Swiftcurrent Lake and Lake Josephine[cite: 3, 4]. Along the way, we crossed a wooden suspension bridge swinging high above a rushing glacial stream—walking across it made us bounce and 'dance' with every step![cite: 3, 4]

Reaching Grinnell Lake was breathtaking[cite: 3, 4]. The water glowed in a striking, opaque turquoise color beneath massive, snow-capped mountain walls[cite: 3, 4]. We dipped our bare feet straight into the freezing, icy water—a shocking but invigorating thrill[cite: 3, 4]. For the return trek, we took the narrow southern loop trail through dense forest[cite: 3, 4]. Not long into the walk, we spotted fresh black bear scat right on the single-file path![cite: 3, 4]

Realizing we were sharing a tight trail with a bear nearby, we immediately sprang into action: clapping our hands loudly, singing songs at the top of our lungs, and playing music from my phone as we quickened our pace[cite: 3, 4]. Spotting multiple puddles of fresh scat kept our hearts racing until we finally broke out of the tree line and reached safety at the lodge, letting out a massive sigh of relief![cite: 3, 4]

Back at Many Glacier Lodge, we celebrated with a hot slice of fresh cheese pizza, enjoyed our packed egg curry and rice at the parking lot, and embarked on the 7.5-hour drive south toward Victor, Idaho[cite: 3, 4]. Crossing into Idaho, a wild black bear suddenly emerged from the side of the road, freezing in our headlights before slinking into the dark[cite: 3, 4]. By 11:30 PM, we pulled into Victor, grabbed Domino's pizza, and collapsed into bed, exhausted[cite: 3, 4].
